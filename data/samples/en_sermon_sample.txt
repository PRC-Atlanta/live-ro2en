Good morning, brothers and sisters. Please open your Bibles to the book of Habakkuk, chapter two. While you are finding the passage, let me tell you why I chose it today.

Habakkuk lived in a dark time. Nebuchadnezzar and the Babylonians were coming, and the prophet could not understand why God would allow it. So he climbed his watchtower and waited. And the Lord answered him: the just shall live by his faith.

That one sentence travels through the whole Bible. Paul quotes it in Romans, and again in Galatians, and the writer of Hebrews quotes it as well. It is the heart of the gospel. We are not made righteous by our own works. Justification is a gift, received by faith in Jesus Christ.

Think about Abraham. Before there was a law, before there was a tabernacle, before Moses ever climbed Mount Sinai, Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness. Think about Melchizedek, the priest of the Most High God, who blessed Abraham with bread and wine. The letter to the Hebrews tells us that Jesus is a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek.

Now, some of you are waiting like Habakkuk this morning. You are standing on the watchtower. You have prayed, and the answer has not come yet. Hear the word of the Lord: the vision is yet for an appointed time. Though it tarry, wait for it, because it will surely come.

In Gethsemane, our Lord prayed, not my will, but yours be done. At Golgotha, he became the propitiation for our sins. And on the third day he rose again. That is why we can sing hallelujah even in the waiting.

So let me ask you three questions. First, where is your watchtower? Second, what are you waiting for? And third, will you live by faith while you wait?

Let us pray. Father, thank you for your faithfulness from Bethlehem to Calvary. Teach us to wait, and teach us to trust. In the name of Jesus, amen.
